# Visual system and adaptation

[中文](design.md)

Complete [style selection](style-selection.en.md) first. There is no global palette. Choose neutral, text and accent colors that fit the direction and brand; keep meaningful text sufficiently contrasted.

For a 1280×720 canvas, a frame around x=64, y=48, width=1152, height=624 is a starting point. Covers may use wider margins. Geometry in pixels is not typography in points: at 96 DPI, 1 pt is about 1.333 px.

Starting sizes: cover 60–76 pt, content title 30–36 pt, point headings 22–24 pt, body 20–24 pt, sources 11–13 pt. A dense table may use 18–20 pt if the viewing context permits. Edit before shrinking. Use about 1.2–1.35 body line spacing; keep essential caveats readable.

Verify installed fonts and language coverage. Microsoft YaHei, DengXian or SimSun may suit Chinese on Windows; verify availability elsewhere. Use clear sans-serif hierarchy for executive/data/technical material, an optional serif for editorial titles, and readable text for teaching. Never claim fonts are embedded without checking.

Choose layouts by content:

- Cover: one subject and a concrete positioning line.
- Argument: one/two columns of explained points, with evidence nearby.
- Comparison: aligned dimensions and a shared baseline.
- Data: a primary chart with necessary units, labels and caveats.
- Action: a table or text rows organized by time/responsibility.

Allocate space to complete information before adjusting whitespace. Using roughly six to seven tenths of the usable region for meaningful content can be a starting point, not a pixel occupancy test. Use compact within-group spacing and clear between-group separation. A text/image split around 55–65% versus 35–45% may work, but evidence determines the balance.

Do not repeat “huge keyword on one half, one sentence on the other” across reporting pages. Do not add unrelated images or repeated claims to fill space. Keep native evidence editable.

Inspect hierarchy in thumbnails and readability at full size. Check alignment, footers, wrapping, crops, rotated bounds and chart color meanings. Fix concrete problems rather than assigning a subjective premium score.
