# Quiet PPT

[完整中文指令](SKILL.zh-CN.md)

Build a clear, credible presentation that a speaker can use without writing the missing argument themselves. Default to editable `.pptx`, useful explanations and per-slide talk tracks. A web animation does not replace a requested native deck.

This is a design workflow, not a template. Annular pictures, warm paper colors, serif titles and wheel transitions are optional treatments.

## Understand the task

Use the supplied material to establish audience, purpose, total slide count, sources and destination. Ask only when missing information materially changes the result. A small unspecified demonstration can use a clearly fictional topic and four slides. Default to a presentation-ready density; switch to sparse keynote slides only when appropriate to the user's explicit purpose.

Inspect supplied templates, brand rules and references first. Extract principles from a reference video without copying its artwork. If it cannot be viewed, say so rather than inferring its contents from its title.

## Match the style first

Read [style selection](references/style-selection.en.md) for a new deck. Identify the audience's decision, evidence types, formality, image quality, density and brand constraints. Respect an explicit template first. Compare the two most suitable directions internally, choose one, and explain briefly. Do not require a style questionnaire when the material already supports a decision.

Executive, research, product/technology, editorial/brand, teaching and architecture/portfolio are adaptable starting points. Change typography, composition, evidence and pace, not just colors.

Keep one main direction through a normal deck, with layouts that vary by evidence. A deliberately grouped sampler may compare directions. Reassess fit before reusing the previous task's style.

## Default to a usable presentation

Read [density and speaker notes](references/reporting.en.md). A typical content slide has a clear title and three to five explained points, or equivalent evidence in a chart, table or image. Include the relevant reason, example or action.

For initial drafting, an ordinary Chinese text-led slide may use about 140–240 characters; a comparable English slide may start at roughly 70–120 words. Reduce prose when evidence carries information. These are flexible guides, not quotas. Never invent facts to reach them. Covers and brief conclusions have different needs.

For each content slide, write natural notes explaining the evidence, implications, conditions and next connection. Start around 180–320 Chinese characters or 110–170 English words, adjusted to actual content and duration. Keep essential facts on the slide too. Follow a user's request to omit notes.

## Narrative and evidence

Identify each slide's subject, main message, evidence, speaking points, layout and purpose of any motion. Use an order that serves the material without forcing every deck into one storyline.

- Give each slide a visual focus and a direct topic title or supported takeaway.
- Preserve units, periods, baselines, sources and limitations. Mark demonstration data visibly.
- Redistribute or edit crowded material within the requested total slide count before adding slides or shrinking text.
- Preserve required facts and caveats. Do not invent business results for dramatic effect.

## Visual principles

Read [visual design](references/design.en.md) as needed after choosing a direction.

- Use 16:9 unless the request/reference requires otherwise. Content margins around 4.5–6% of slide width are a starting point.
- Choose colors from the direction, brand and evidence. Keep color meanings consistent; do not impose a paper/green palette.
- Use available fonts with clear hierarchy and required language glyphs.
- Allocate area to evidence and explanation. Avoid huge decorative keywords on ordinary pages, repetitive UI card walls and meaningless labels.
- Use native charts for comparisons, images when relevant, and editable tables/diagrams when called for. Do not make every page a three-box layout.
- Text-led pages can work. Do not fill space with unrelated stock artwork.

## Choose motion independently

Read [motion and compatibility](references/motion.en.md) when useful or requested. Default to no transition or a short fade on ordinary pages. Use Morph to preserve object identity across views or stages.

Use annular/wheel treatments only when requested, supported by meaningful cycles/multiple views, or accepted in a preview. Keep body copy horizontal. A moving diagram focus or continuous image crop may communicate better than rotation.

Write the effect into the PPTX or disclose what remains unimplemented. Static renders, PDFs and browser demos do not establish native playback. Keep readable end states and acknowledge player differences.

## Author, review and deliver

Use an available editable-presentation authoring tool. If the host has a `presentations` skill, follow its APIs, export and review procedure. Do not replace editable pages with screenshots.

Render and inspect every slide. Fix clipping, wrapping, unintended overlap, low contrast and chart/data discrepancies. Keep references in relevant notes and material caveats visible. Check structure, slide count, fonts, editability and Morph matching. Claim native playback validation only after inspecting playback or a native export.

Before delivery, verify:

1. The main subject and supporting explanation are understandable.
2. The audience can read the text at presentation size.
3. Colors, graphics and effects have a role.
4. Hierarchy and alignment stay consistent.
5. Evidence is traceable and required objects are editable.
6. Content works without motion.
7. The speaker can use slides and notes without inventing missing reasoning.
8. Useful explanations were not removed merely to create whitespace.
9. Style follows this task rather than the last sample.

Deliver the requested deck and a short explanation of material limitations. Add a preview when useful, not a validation log. Improve hierarchy, alignment, evidence and imagery before adding decoration in response to “make it more premium.” User feedback takes priority over aesthetic defaults.
